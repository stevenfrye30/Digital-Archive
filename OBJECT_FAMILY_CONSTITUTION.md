# Object Family Constitution

*Compiled 2026-05-22. The first sustained articulation of what
each witness family IS in the archive, what its constitutional
role is, and what visual/manuscript behaviors should distinguish
it from sibling families. Companion to
`WITNESS_FAMILY_CONSTITUTION.md`, `LEAF_CONSTITUTION_AUDIT.md`,
and the prior Genesis-arc documents.*

This constitution turns the Atlas Object-family system from
*styled variants of a generic modal* into *distinct manuscript
traditions sharing one civilizational volume*. The pass before
this (Leaf Constitution) shipped commentary, linguistic, and
architecture as recognizable manuscript traditions. This pass
extends that doctrine to five further families that the
priority brief named, and writes the full register down so
future families can grow into clear constitutional positions.

---

## Part I — Constitutional roles

The archive carries a small number of *kinds* of witness, each
playing a different role in the long memory of a verse. Each
family answers a different question. The family's identity is
the question it answers.

### Already-mature families

**Commentary** — *transmitted intellectual witness*.
The cumulative interpretive tradition: Patristic, Scholastic,
Reformation, Modern. The witness that says *what has been
thought*. Reads as: argument unfolding in a measured column.

**Linguistic / philological** — *gloss witness*.
The marginal note tradition that anchors a single original-
language word and traces its grammar, recurrence, and reading.
Reads as: lexeme foregrounded, gloss attended to.

**Architecture / sacred-site** — *land-memory witness*.
The patriarchal landscape's accumulated places — altars, wells,
pillars, caves, mountains. Reads as: inscription, held in
small voice.

**Doré (plate)** — *visual interpretive witness*.
Nineteenth-century engraved illustration. Visual gravity. Reads
as: monumental, framed by the dark chamber.

**Genealogy chains** — *descent register witness*.
The patriarchal narrative's lineage backbone. Reads as: tree
form, indented by generation.

**Atlas Object chambers (×12)** — *bespoke encounter
architecture*. Twelve realized chamber renderers, each a
unique world (cosmology, sanctuary, lament, etc.). Distinct
by design.

### Priority-this-pass families

**Reception-history** — *historical afterlife witness*.
The chronology of a passage's readings across centuries and
traditions. Patristic, Rabbinic, Islamic, Modern.
The witness that says *how this verse has been read*.
Reads as: layered chronology, multiple traditions speaking in
turn, each its own moment.

**Manuscript** — *textual evidence witness*.
The critical apparatus — Hebrew variants, LXX, Vulgate,
Masoretic vocalization, Samaritan, Qumran. The witness that
says *what the text actually says, where readings divide*.
Reads as: technical, dense, precise. A footnote register, not
a paragraph register.

**Ritual / liturgical** — *lived witness*.
The practice the verse generates: blessing, festival, daily
prayer, cantillation. The witness that says *how this verse is
lived*. Reads as: liturgical breath, spoken cadence, prayer-
shaped spacing.

**Cross-reference** — *canonical resonance witness*.
The list of other scripture passages this verse touches.
The witness that says *where else this voice is heard in the
canon*. Reads as: a sparse vertical list, marginal-column
shape, list-shape rather than paragraph-shape.

**Cosmology (non-chamber)** — *world-model witness*.
The cosmos this verse assumes — ANE three-tier model, ancient
sky-vault, post-flood ordering. The witness that says *what
kind of world this verse is built inside*. Reads as: world-
painted prose, slightly wider breath, the cosmos sketched in
language rather than diagram.

### Deferred families (one or two records each, identity not
yet stabilized)

- **Timeline / calendar** — *temporal-pattern witness*. The
  calendar this verse founds. Currently 1 record (Gen 1:14).
- **Artifact** — *material-culture witness*. The objects the
  ANE world contained. Currently 1 record (tannînim, Gen 1:21).
- **Reconstruction** — *source-critical witness*. The
  hypothesized literary stratum (Priestly, Yahwist, etc.).
  Currently 1 record (Gen 1:27).
- **Audio** — *performed witness*. Cantillation, sung tradition.
  Currently 1 record (Gen 1:31).
- **Map / itinerary** — *geographic witness*. The land moved
  through. Currently 2 records (Gen 1:14 constellations, Gen
  12:5 patriarchal journeys).
- **Translation-tradition** — chamber form for AO·002. Not a
  default-leaf kind.
- **Symbolic-motif, covenant-formula, etc.** — AO chamber
  forms. Each is its own chamber renderer.

These six (timeline, artifact, reconstruction, audio, map, and
the unchamberred remainder) remain in default chrome until
their record counts cross five and their constitutional voice
has had time to crystallize editorially. The constitution's
principle holds: families crystallize when they reach five
records, and not before — except where the brief explicitly
names a priority (as it does for manuscript, ritual, and
cross-reference at one record each).

## Part II — Visual / manuscript behaviors

Each family's identity lives in measure, typography, and
rhythm. Never in chrome, badges, color chips, or modern UI
affordances.

The full register, post-this-pass:

| Family             | Body width | Body em | Line-hgt | Opacity | Title voice                          | Verse-ref      | Signature |
|--------------------|-----------:|--------:|---------:|--------:|--------------------------------------|----------------|-----------|
| Default            |    700 px  |   0.89  |    1.72  |  0.86   | italic 500, 1.18 em                  | 14.4 px        | broad archival; no ornament |
| Cosmology          |    620 px  |   0.91  |    1.70  |  0.92   | italic 500, 1.14 em                  | 14.4 px        | world-painted prose, slight body presence |
| Commentary         |    640 px  |   0.92  |    1.74  |  0.78   | italic 400, 1.18 em                  | 14.4 px        | 36 px centred hairline rule |
| Reception-history  |    600 px  |   0.90  |    1.68  |  0.88   | italic 500, 1.14 em                  | 14.4 px        | wider spacers between paragraphs (tradition divider rhythm) |
| Linguistic         |    560 px  |   0.88  |    1.66  |  0.78   | roman 500, 1.08 em                   | 12.16 px       | dense paragraph rhythm (0.5 em spacer) |
| Ritual             |    560 px  |   0.89  |    1.78  |  0.88   | italic 400, 1.10 em                  | 14.4 px        | prayer-breath line-height; quiet title; centred opening line |
| Cross-reference    |    540 px  |   0.87  |    1.54  |  0.92   | italic 500, 1.06 em                  | 14.4 px        | tight, list-shaped, narrow spacer |
| Manuscript         |    500 px  |   0.84  |    1.55  |  0.92   | roman 500, 1.04 em, letter-spaced    | 11.2 px        | critical-apparatus density; smallest measure & font |
| Architecture       |    480 px  |   0.90  |    1.60  |  0.78   | italic 500, 1.10 em, **small-caps**  | letter-spaced  | inscription; engraved verse attribution; sealing-line cadence |

The cadence is intentional. Reading across the rows, the four
existing-mature families anchor the corners (commentary widest
argument, linguistic dense gloss, architecture smallest
inscription, default broad archival). The five new families
fill the interior:

- **Cosmology** sits widest among the new five — its prose
  paints a world and needs room.
- **Reception-history** sits between commentary and linguistic
  — substantive but more concentrated, paragraph-by-paragraph
  by tradition.
- **Ritual** sits at linguistic's measure but uses the widest
  line-height in the codex (1.78) — prayer's breath.
- **Cross-reference** is narrower still, with the tightest
  line-height — a list, not a paragraph.
- **Manuscript** is the narrowest text-witness (500 px); its
  font is the smallest of all (0.84 em); critical apparatus
  density. The verse-ref is also smallest (11.2 px) — apparatus
  footnotes carry tiny citations.

This produces a nine-step measure gradient
(700 → 640 → 620 → 600 → 560 → 540 → 500 → 480),
each family at its own column-width with its own line-height,
title, and rhythm. The codex's reader, encountering one of
each, will increasingly recognise the kind before reading the
content.

## Part III — Embodiment register

For each priority-this-pass family, which Genesis records
currently exist, and which embody the family's character
versus which still feel under-developed.

### Reception-history (4 records, above-threshold soon)

- **gen.1.2** `gen1-reception-ruach` — rûaḥ ʾĕlōhîm reception
  across Christian, Jewish, modern readings. *Embodies the
  family well.* Three-fold chronological structure already in
  the prose.
- **gen.1.27** `gen1-reception-imago-history` — Imago Dei
  reception across 2,000 years (Irenaeus → moderns). *Embodies
  the family well.* The "imago across centuries" structure is
  exemplary.
- **gen.18.2** `gen18-three-visitors-reception` — Christian
  patristic + Jewish angelic + Eastern Orthodox icon readings
  of Mamre. *Embodies the family well.* Three traditions, each
  its own paragraph.
- **gen.22.19** `gen22-reception-three-traditions` — Akedah in
  Jewish, Christian, Islamic readings. *Embodies the family
  exceptionally well.* The "three-traditions" structure is
  the family's canonical form.

All four records carry the family's character editorially. The
weakness is visual: they all render in default chrome.

### Manuscript (1 record)

- **gen.1.1** `gen1-manuscript-opening` — Manuscript witnesses
  to Gen 1:1, listing Masoretic, LXX, Samaritan, Vulgate
  readings with vocalization notes. *Embodies the family
  well* but is the only example.

This family is single-record but the brief names it a priority.
The CSS this pass establishes will be inherited by future
manuscript witnesses as the textual-evidence apparatus grows.

### Ritual / liturgical (2 records, one of which is a chamber)

- **gen.1.3** `gen1-ritual-maariv-aravim` — Hebrew evening
  blessing, the daily liturgical witness woven around the
  creation of light. *Embodies the family well.*
- **gen.2.1** `gen2-sabbath-pattern` — AO·004 chamber; renders
  through bespoke `_renderSabbathChamber`. *Chamber, not
  default leaf.* The new ritual-family CSS must NOT touch
  the sabbath chamber; the constitution preserves chamber
  identity above family identity.

The CSS uses `:not(.sabbath-chamber)` to exclude the AO chamber.

### Cross-reference (1 record)

- **gen.1.1** `gen1-xref-nt-creation` — New Testament echoes
  of Genesis 1:1 (John 1, Hebrews 1, Colossians 1, Revelation
  21). *Embodies the family well.* Format already list-shaped.

This family is also single-record-but-priority. Future cross-
reference witnesses will inherit the constitutional voice.

### Cosmology (3 records, two of which are non-chamber)

- **gen.1.1** `gen1-cosmology-ane` — Ancient Near Eastern
  cosmology, the three-tier model the text inherits. *Embodies
  the family well.*
- **gen.1.3** `gen1-cosmology-light-before-luminaries` — the
  puzzle of light created before sun/moon/stars. *Embodies the
  family well.*
- **gen.1.6** `gen1-cosmology-firmament` — AO·001 chamber.
  Chamber, not default leaf. The new cosmology-family CSS
  excludes `.cosmology-chamber`.

## Part V — What this pass does NOT do

Per the brief, explicitly:

- **No flashy animations**, no card UI, no colorful interfaces,
  no dashboard aesthetics, no excessive ornament, no fake
  texture overlays, no fantasy RPG styling, no museum-kiosk
  energy.
- **No new Atlas Objects.** Twelve. The constitution holds.
- **No content changes.** The 4 reception-history records are
  editorially complete; the 1 manuscript record likewise; the
  1 ritual record (ma'ariv) likewise; the 1 cross-reference
  likewise; the 2 cosmology records likewise. The CSS works on
  what is already there.
- **No move to Exodus.** Genesis remains the proving ground.
- **No formalization of deferred families** (timeline, artifact,
  reconstruction, audio, map). They remain in default chrome
  until their record counts cross five.

## Part VI — Why each family now feels more distinct

After this pass, a reader summoning a sequence of leaves will
encounter:

- **Cosmology**: wider breathing column with slight body
  presence — the prose feels like it is painting a world,
  not pointing at one. The reader senses *the cosmos this verse
  assumes.*
- **Reception-history**: substantive column with wide spacers
  between paragraphs — each spacer reads as a chronological
  divider; each paragraph as a tradition speaking. The reader
  senses *the long reading.*
- **Ritual**: prayer-breath line-height (1.78, the codex's
  widest) — the prose reads with the spoken cadence of
  liturgy. The reader senses *the practice.*
- **Cross-reference**: narrow tight column with the codex's
  smallest line-height — the prose reads as a vertical list,
  marginal-column-shape. The reader senses *the canonical
  echoes.*
- **Manuscript**: smallest column, smallest font, smallest
  verse-ref, roman letter-spaced title — the leaf reads as a
  critical apparatus footnote, not a study Bible page. The
  reader senses *the textual evidence.*

Plus the three formalized families holding their positions:
- **Commentary**: 640 px italic-400 with hairline rule, argument
  flowing.
- **Linguistic**: 560 px roman 500, the lexeme foregrounded.
- **Architecture**: 480 px small-caps, the inscription.

Plus Doré preserved as the museum chamber.

Nine recognizable manuscript-tradition voices, one codex.

## Posture

Genesis emerges from this pass as the first truly inhabited
region of the archive. Each verse that carries witnesses
carries them in their own kind of writing — commentary
arguing, linguistic glossing, architecture inscribing, ritual
praying, reception layering, manuscript apparatus, cross-
reference listing, cosmology world-painting. The default
chrome remains the holding-pattern for un-formed families.
The chambers remain bespoke.

The codex is no longer a stack of identically-rendered
modals. It is beginning to read as a working archive of
distinct manuscript traditions.

The work this pass requires is small in CSS but large in
constitutional weight — it writes down, for the first time,
what each kind of witness *is* in the archive. Future passes
inherit this register. Future records inherit these
constitutional voices.

The next time a family reaches five records and the
constitution turns its attention to it, this document will
be the editorial baseline.
