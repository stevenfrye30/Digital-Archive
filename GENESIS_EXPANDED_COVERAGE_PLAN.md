# Genesis — Expanded Coverage Plan

*Compiled 2026-05-20. Editorial and architectural judgment only.
A philosophical refinement of the Maturation Roadmap, written
after the v58-v65 system maturation. No implementation in this
phase.*

This document sits beside, and partly revises, two earlier
constitutional documents:

  · `ATLAS_EDITORIAL_AUDIT.md` — the codex's first governing
    doctrine, written when the archive crossed from experimental
    feature work into civilizational manuscript.

  · `GENESIS_MATURATION_ROADMAP.md` — the chapter-by-chapter
    audit of Genesis coverage and the twenty proposed
    additions, drafted when patriarchal Genesis was still a
    textual desert.

Wave One of the Roadmap has now shipped (six records spanning
gen.12-46). Five subsequent system passes — silhouette
hierarchy (v58-v59), chapter-plate restoration (v61-v62),
folio header centring and top traverse (v63), the Atlas Index
(v64-v65) — have materially changed what coverage *means* in
the codex.

This document records the philosophical shift those passes
made possible, and revises Genesis strategy accordingly.

---

## I. The shift — why silence can now soften

### What the older doctrine said

The Editorial Audit treated silence as a hard prohibition.
Four architectures were named as "should remain silent"
permanently — the divine council, martyrdom, pilgrim ascent,
and certain devotional psalms. The Maturation Roadmap echoed
this discipline at the chapter level: twenty Genesis chapters
were marked "should remain silent" alongside the proposed
additions. The Wave One Plan reserved most of the great
patriarchal moments (Akedah, Peniel, Joseph dreams, three
visitors, Melchizedek, covenant of pieces) for later phases.

The reason was sound at the time. Before silhouette
differentiation, before the Atlas Index, before the folio
toggle worked the way it now does, **any addition was visible
the same way every other addition was visible**. A tertiary
linguistic gloss at gen.4.10 and a six-record realized chamber
at gen.1 both surfaced as marks in the same rail at the same
weight at the same hover behaviour. A new record was, in
practice, **a new interruption**.

Restraint was therefore the only defensible posture. The
codex's editorial voice had to be limited because the codex
had no way to *vary the volume* of its voice.

### What the system passes changed

Six material changes between v58 and v65 reshape the question:

1. **v58 — tertiary opacity legibility.** The "disabled UI"
   reading dissolved; tertiary marks are now legibly quiet
   without reading as broken.

2. **v59 — silhouette separation.** Tertiary boxes are smaller
   (26×26 vs 28×28), glyphs lighter (13px vs 15-16px), borders
   thinner (1px dotted vs 1.5px dashed/solid), parchment cooler
   (grey vs warm). A tertiary mark *no longer competes with a
   secondary or primary mark* at glance distance — they read
   as different shapes, not different alphas.

3. **v59 — cluster breathing.** Tertiary-only clusters now sit
   with 24px collision-gap rather than 14px. Quiet stretches of
   the rail breathe; the eye reads them as marginal glosses
   orbiting the verse, not as competing items.

4. **v61-v62 — chapter-plate restoration.** Scripture now
   opens with a ceremonial small-caps + Roman-numeral plate
   before any verse. The reader feels the witness *open*; the
   rail's marks no longer compete with the opening of the
   chapter, because the chapter has its own typographic
   sovereignty.

5. **v63 — folio mode and top traverse.** The reader can
   collapse the rail entirely via Pure mode; chapter-to-chapter
   navigation works without scrolling. **Coverage that exists in
   the codex no longer compels itself on the reader.**

6. **v64-v65 — the Atlas Index.** A separate consultation
   surface — a manuscript witness ledger — where coverage can
   be enumerated, browsed, and reached without disturbing
   reading. The codex can now *record where it has spoken*
   without having to *speak everywhere it has recorded*.

The cumulative effect is a hierarchy that scales. A chapter
can now carry one secondary mark and two or three tertiary
marks without crowding scripture, because each tier behaves
differently in the rail and the rail itself can be dismissed.

### What this means for Genesis

The earlier doctrine of *preserve large silent regions* was
born of a constraint that has now eased. It is not repealed —
silence is still part of the architecture — but it is no
longer the **default** posture for any chapter that bears
canonical weight. The new default is closer to the manuscript
tradition itself: every major moment receives at least a
quiet gloss; ordinary narrative connective tissue rests in
silence; only certain extra-narrative architectures (the
divine council, pilgrim ascent, martyrdom) remain off-limits
on interpretive grounds.

The shift, named plainly:

  *from* "preserve large silent regions"
  *to* "preserve rhythmic pacing while ensuring major
        moments are eventually witnessed."

Restraint is now expressed in **hierarchy** (most marks
tertiary, fewer secondary, fewer still chambers) rather than
in **absence**.

## II. The discipline that must not soften

This shift creates real dangers that must be named before
expansion begins.

### 1. The encyclopedic temptation

Once the hierarchy permits broader inhabitation, the editorial
temptation will be to inhabit *everywhere*. The Editorial
Audit's central insight remains true: Atlas succeeds because
it remains manuscriptal rather than computational. A Bible
that carries a record at every verse is a database, not a
manuscript. The new posture **expands the permitted territory,
not the permitted density**.

A chapter that gains one tertiary mark where previously it had
none is now inhabited. It does not need three. The discipline
is: *one mark suffices to acknowledge a chapter*. Two marks
together (paired vision + pillar at Gen 28; altar + call at
Gen 12) are the cadence pattern Wave One established and
should remain the norm. Three or more marks at a single chapter
should always be a deliberate chamber decision, not a drift.

### 2. The hierarchy drift

Three tiers exist; their distinctions are load-bearing.
Promoting a tertiary record to secondary because "it feels
important" erodes the rail's legibility. Every author of a new
record must check: is this a *quiet gloss*, a *substantial
scholarly witness*, or a *canonical anchor*? The Atlas Index
classification line will be the first test — if "Tertiary ·
Commentary" feels demeaning for a record, the temptation will
be to write something more — but the right answer is usually
that the gloss should remain a gloss.

### 3. The chamber inflation

The codex currently has six realized chambers (the five named
in the Editorial Audit, plus the patriarchal mode established
by Wave One). A realized chamber is a substantial editorial
commitment. The Akedah chamber is still the right next chamber.
The third should wait until the Akedah has lived. Inflating
to ten or fifteen chambers within Genesis alone would tip the
book back toward the Genesis 1 cliff problem the Editorial
Audit flagged.

### 4. The verse-per-chapter ceiling

The two-AO ceiling per verse — established by the Editorial
Audit and re-affirmed in v59 — remains permanent. No verse
should ever carry three AO markers. The expanded coverage
plan respects this absolutely; AO recurrence anchors should
still be added with great deliberation, never to "complete a
pattern."

### 5. The Dinah principle

Some narrative passages resist editorial commentary not
because they lack importance, but because the codex's
interpretive voice would worsen them. Gen 34 (Dinah) is the
clearest example. Adding apparatus there does not honour the
text; it intrudes upon a narrative the canon itself preserves
with deliberate ethical austerity. **The hierarchy expansion
does not lift the Dinah principle.** Certain chapters' silence
remains the editor's right answer.

## III. Witness scales — six classes of addition

The expanded plan thinks at six distinct scales of inhabitation.
Knowing which scale to use is the central editorial discipline.

### Scale 1 — tertiary gloss

A single record, dotted 26×26 mark in the rail, paragraph or
two of inscription-shaped prose when opened. The smallest unit
of acknowledgement. Sufficient to mark a chapter as inhabited.

  Example: `gen.4.10 — blood that cries from the ground`
  Example: `gen.16.13 — el-roi, "the God who sees me"`

### Scale 2 — secondary commentary

A single record at the substantive scholarly register. Dashed
28×28 mark. Three to four paragraphs when opened. Used for
*moments the codex must actually say something about*: the
call (gen.12.1), the ladder (gen.28.12), the wrestling
(gen.32.24).

  Example: `gen.14.18 — Melchizedek`
  Example: `gen.18.2 — three visitors (reception history)`

### Scale 3 — map or plate

A single visual record — a Doré-equivalent engraving or a
single map plate. Distinct from textual commentary in
materiality. The codex currently carries five Doré plates,
all in Gen 1-11. Expansion into the patriarchal section is
*possible but discouraged for the Doré style*; a Rembrandt at
the Akedah or a Rublev icon at the three visitors would be
in a different register (more deliberate, less ornamental).

  Example: `gen.12.5 — map of patriarchal journeys`

### Scale 4 — visual witness

A reproduction-class record (manuscript folio, codex page,
inscription, sculpture). Carries a primary or secondary
authority depending on its provenance. Rare; reserved for
moments where the visual artifact itself is the witness.

  Example: hypothetical — a Masoretic manuscript leaf for
           gen.1.1; or the Tablet of Atrahasis for gen.6-9.

### Scale 5 — genealogy or recurrence-anchor witness

A single record that marks a *return* of an existing AO at a
new verse, or that extends an existing genealogy chamber to
a new branch. These records do not add new ontology — they
extend the reach of objects that already exist.

  Example: `AO·002 (translation tradition) recurs at gen.11.7`
           — the Babel confusion of tongues as a recurrence
           of the translation lineage.
  Example: A Davidic genealogy chain at a future verse, when
           the canon's lineage system is extended beyond
           Genesis.

### Scale 6 — possible future chamber

A multi-record realized chamber, like Genesis 1 or Exodus 40.
Six to twelve records in a deliberate apparatus arrangement.
At present, exactly one such chamber is planned: the Akedah
at Gen 22. No second patriarchal chamber should be designed
until the Akedah has lived.

  Example: `gen.22 — the Akedah` (six records, planned)

The distinction between Scale 1 (single tertiary gloss) and
Scale 6 (multi-record chamber) is roughly fifty-fold in
editorial weight. The expanded coverage plan uses Scale 1
liberally, Scales 2-5 carefully, and Scale 6 only once.

## IV. Genesis reassessed chapter by chapter

Below, each Genesis chapter is reconsidered in light of the
softened doctrine. The format:

```
Gen N — short title
  Current state: what is present today
  Posture: silent · light · acknowledged · realized · chamber
  Proposed scales: (1 / 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 / 6) ×count
  Notes: brief editorial reasoning
```

The "posture" line names the intended inhabitation level after
expansion:

- **silent** — no records, intentionally
- **light** — one tertiary record only
- **acknowledged** — two to three records, mixed tiers
- **realized** — four or more records or a small chamber
- **chamber** — a multi-record realized chamber

## V. Synthesis — proposed Genesis coverage shape

After expansion, Genesis would carry roughly:

- **Realized chambers:** 2 (Gen 1 cosmology, Gen 22 Akedah —
  the latter reserved for a future wave)
- **Realized lineage chamber:** 1 (Gen 5 antediluvian line)
- **Secondary commentary records:** ~14
- **Tertiary records:** ~30-32
- **Genealogy chains:** 11 (unchanged)
- **Doré plates:** 5 (unchanged)
- **Map plate:** 1 (gen.12.5, optional)
- **Codex Object originating anchors:** 6 (unchanged)
- **Codex Object recurrence anchors (potential):**
  + AO·006 reconsidered to originate at gen.9.9 (Noahide) —
    separate deliberation
  + AO·002 recurrence at gen.11.7 (Babel) — separate
    deliberation

Total Genesis presence: approximately **70-80 records across
50 chapters**, vs the current 48 anchor entries.

Coverage at the chapter level:

```
Chambers / realized:     Gen 1, 5, 22 (eventual)
Acknowledged:            Gen 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 9, 12, 14, 15, 17,
                         18, 19, 21, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29, 32,
                         35, 36, 37, 45, 46, 48, 49, 50
Light:                   Gen 8, 10, 11, 13, 16, 23, 24, 33,
                         38, 39, 40, 41
Silent (permanent):      Gen 20, 30, 31, 34, 42, 43, 44, 47
```

**Silent chapters reduced from ~20 to 8.** All eight silences
are now editorial choices, not coverage gaps. Gen 34 (Dinah)
remains silent on the Dinah principle. Gen 30, 31 remain
silent because household-rivalry narratives don't bear
apparatus. Gen 42-44 remain silent to honour the dramatic
crescendo of the brother-recognition arc; Gen 45 carries the
break. Gen 47 stays silent because Gen 49's blessing theology
is the focal point. Gen 20 stays silent as the dramatic intake
of breath between Sodom and Isaac's birth.

## VI. Pacing — what the expanded Genesis should FEEL like

The Maturation Roadmap distinguished two registers:
**primordial architecture** (Gen 1-11) and **wandering memory**
(Gen 12-50). The Wave One Plan established that distinction
in implementation.

The expanded plan does not collapse those registers — it
deepens each.

### Gen 1-11 — primeval, now fully inhabited

The Gen 1 cliff problem fades when Gen 2-11 each carry their
own modest apparatus. Gen 3 with its protoevangelium reading.
Gen 4 with its first lyric. Gen 6 with the Nephilim gloss. Gen
9 with the Noahide covenant as the canon's first formal
covenant. Gen 10 with the seventy nations. Gen 11 with the
babel/balal wordplay.

The reader passing from Gen 1 to Gen 11 no longer experiences
a sudden silence — instead, an apparatus that *narrows from
encyclopedic to attentive* across the primeval narrative,
matching the canon's own progression from cosmic to
genealogical.

### Gen 12-50 — wandering memory, with proper landmarks

Wave One established the patriarchal grammar: small `⌂` marks
at altar / well / pillar; quiet `❡` commentary at the call,
the ladder, the descent. The expanded plan extends this rhythm:

- Three sacred-site marks become **seven or eight** as Gen 13,
  Gen 23, Gen 26, Gen 35, Gen 38 receive their architectural
  glosses.
- Two narrative-arc commentary marks become **eight or nine**
  as Gen 14, 15, 18, 27, 32, 37, 45, 49 each receive their
  reflective gloss.
- Two visionary moments (gen.28 ladder + gen.32 wrestling)
  form a quiet thread; gen.37 dreams adds a third; gen.41
  Pharaoh adds a fourth.

The reader moving through Genesis 12-50 experiences a **steady
pilgrimage cadence**: every two to three chapters, a quiet
mark in the margin acknowledges the passage. No chapter is a
desert. No chapter is a museum. The codex walks beside the
reader, marking what is important and falling silent where
the canon itself prefers austerity.

### The chamber moments

Of the 50 chapters, only two carry chambers — the Gen 1
cosmology chamber and the (eventual) Gen 22 Akedah chamber.
Twenty chapters fall between them; thirty chapters fall
after. The cadence: one chamber roughly every 20-25 chapters,
which is the right cadence for the codex as a whole if the
canon is to be inhabited consistently.

## VII. The AO recurrence question

Two AO recurrence considerations emerged from this reassessment.
Both are flagged here but not committed to.

### AO·006 (Covenant Formula) — re-anchoring consideration

The Covenant Formula currently originates at gen.17.7 ("I will
be your God; you shall be my people"). The Noahide covenant at
gen.9.9 is, strictly, **the canon's first explicit covenant**.
A re-anchoring would treat:

  - gen.9.9 as the primal originating
  - gen.17.7 as the patriarchal recurrence
  - exo.6.7 as the Mosaic recurrence
  - jer.31.33 as the new-covenant recurrence
  - rev.21.3 as the eschatological consummation

This is a metadata change with editorial weight. The current
gen.17.7 origin reflects the formula's *first explicit
verbal form*; gen.9.9 has the covenant act but not the
formula. The choice between *where the object originates*
and *where the formula crystallizes* deserves its own
deliberation, not a decision folded into this plan.

### AO·002 (Translation Tradition) — Babel recurrence

The translation-tradition AO currently anchors at gen.1.6,
eze.1.22, psa.148.4. Babel (gen.11.7) is, civilizationally,
the *originating moment of translation-as-necessity* —
the canon's account of why translation traditions exist at
all. Adding gen.11.7 as a recurrence anchor would deepen the
AO without changing its origin.

This is a less consequential change than the AO·006
question and could be folded into a future wave with less
deliberation. Recommend revisit when Wave Two is being
designed.

## VIII. What still merits restraint

The expansion plan is generous, but eight chapters remain
silent on principled grounds. Restating, with the editorial
reason:

- **Gen 20 — Abimelech doublet.** Silent because the chapter
  is dramatic breath between Sodom and Isaac's birth.
- **Gen 30 — household rivalry, births.** Silent because the
  narrative is rivalry-tissue, not theological emphasis.
- **Gen 31 — Laban, teraphim, departure.** Silent (or one
  optional tertiary on teraphim) because the household drama
  doesn't bear apparatus.
- **Gen 34 — Dinah.** Silent **permanently**, on the Dinah
  principle. The canon's restraint here is right; the codex
  should not improve a passage that honours its subject by
  remaining austere.
- **Gen 42, 43, 44 — brother-recognition build.** Silent to
  honour the dramatic crescendo; Gen 45 carries the break.
- **Gen 47 — famine administration, Pharaoh blessing.** Silent
  because Gen 49 carries the blessing-theology focal point.

These eight silences are now the architecture, not the
default. They are deliberate negative space.

## IX. The forty-record question

The Roadmap proposed twenty additions. Wave One shipped six.
This plan implicitly proposes roughly **thirty to thirty-five
more** across Genesis, bringing the total Genesis presence to
seventy or eighty records when complete.

Is that too many?

The Editorial Audit's central anchoring number was **fifteen
Codex Objects total** across the entire canon. That number is
unchanged — this plan adds *no new Codex Objects to Genesis*
beyond the three already-considered candidates (AO·013-015
remain governed by the Audit's discipline).

For commentary records, the relevant question is not absolute
count but **per-chapter density**. The plan keeps the
per-chapter density bounded:

- Most acknowledged chapters carry **two to three records**
- Light chapters carry **one record**
- Silent chapters carry **zero**

The Gen 1 chamber (twenty records) and the eventual Gen 22
Akedah chamber (six records) are the only chapters that
exceed this density. The contrast between chambers and
ordinary chapters is preserved.

Seventy or eighty records across fifty chapters averages
roughly **1.4 records per chapter** — close to the manuscript
codex tradition itself, where a chapter typically carries one
or two marginal glosses per page.

This is *manuscript density*, not *database density*. The
plan is generous, not encyclopedic.

## X. Closing posture

Genesis is the codex's editorial proving ground. Wave One
proved that the patriarchal section could be inhabited
without losing the cliff-to-cliff atmospheric distinction
between primordial and patriarchal. The system passes that
followed (v58-v65) proved that the codex's hierarchy can now
carry broader coverage without collapsing.

The doctrine, plainly:

  *Silence is no longer the default. Discipline is.*

A chapter that bears canonical weight should usually carry
at least one quiet mark. A chapter that bears no canonical
weight, or whose subject the canon itself treats with
austerity, may rightly remain silent. The choice is
chapter-by-chapter, made by an editor reading the text — not
by a feature planner counting cells.

What this plan promises:

- Genesis fully inhabited at the chapter level: 42 chapters
  carrying at least one mark, 8 chapters left silent
  deliberately.
- Hierarchy preserved: most marks tertiary, fewer secondary,
  exactly two chambers across the book.
- Pacing preserved: a quiet pilgrimage cadence, not an
  apparatus barrage.
- Restraint preserved where restraint is the answer.

What this plan does not promise:

- Annotation density approaching the Gen 1 chamber across
  the canon.
- AO inflation. The fifteen-object ceiling holds.
- A new system, dashboard, or feature surface. The
  hierarchy *already in place* is what makes expansion
  safe.

The codex should now feel, when Genesis is read in full,
like a manuscript codex with deep editorial care expressed
through *attention to moments*, not through *coverage of
verses*. Most chapters whisper. A few chapters speak.
Two chambers sing.

That is what canonical maturity looks like, and that is
what the next phase of Genesis is for.
