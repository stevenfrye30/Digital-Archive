The Map of Modern & New Religions

The new religions born since 1800 — the Latter Day Saints, Christian Science, the Adventists, the Spiritualists and Theosophists, and the global movements of the twentieth century. Coloured by whether a worldwide-public-domain English edition exists.

A new faith is not a closed book. Unlike the ancient scriptures, these were written within living memory — yet most are already public-domain, because their founders wrote in English and died before 1955: the Book of Mormon (1830), Science and Health (1875), Ellen White, Blavatsky, Kardec. The line falls almost exactly at the institutional twentieth century: the amber and red belong to movements whose scripture is corporately controlled (the New World Translation, Scientology, the Divine Principle) — or, for the Japanese and Vietnamese new religions (Tenrikyo, Cao Đài), whose source text is old and public-domain but whose only English translation is a modern copyright. This is the mirror of the Bahá’í door, whose authorized translator died in 1957 and left it nearly all amber. Rastafari lives on the Indigenous door; the Ahmadiyya reform of Islam on the Abrahamic door.
