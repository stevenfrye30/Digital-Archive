Green classics, two different edges

James Legge single-handedly greened the classical canon. His Victorian translations put the entire Confucian canon — the Four Books and Five Classics — and the Daoist classics (Dao De Jing, Zhuangzi) into the public domain, so the philosophical core glows green. But past it, two edges darken: Neo-Confucianism goes amber (Zhu Xi, Wang Yangming — modern ©), and the Daozang — the ~1,500-text Daoist Canon — is a red untranslated ocean, a Daoist parallel to the Buddhist Tengyur. The shared spine is the I Ching: a Confucian Classic and the cosmological root of Daoist alchemy. Tap ▸ open on a teaching to walk in.
