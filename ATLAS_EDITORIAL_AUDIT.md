# Atlas — Editorial Audit and Canon-Completion Roadmap

*Compiled 2026-05-20, after v57 (Biblical Corpus Deepening, first wave).*

This is the codex's first governing editorial doctrine. It is not a feature plan. It is a manuscript editor's read of the codex at the moment it crossed from experimental phase into civilizational manuscript. It exists to slow the next phase, to refuse premature additions, to set the threshold for future Atlas Objects, and to identify — with restraint — what Atlas still owes the Bible.

Silence is part of the architecture. Where this document recommends restraint, that recommendation has the same weight as where it identifies a missing object.

The goal of the next year is not maximum annotation density. The goal is **inevitability**: every Atlas Object, every commentary leaf, every silence should feel as if the codex could not have been built any other way.

### 1.2 Resonance constellation (eight curated pairs)

The resonance map is small by design. Genealogy is intentionally **isolated** — the codex does not force every object into a relation.

```
Sanctuary  ⟷  Incarnation         (presence inhabiting place / flesh)
Sanctuary  ⟷  Apocalypse           (sanctuary disclosed in vision)
Sanctuary  ⟷  Mountain             (elevation crystallized into architecture)
Lament     ⟷  Resurrection         (rupture held / rupture transformed)
Lament     ⟷  Wisdom               (contemplation under unresolved suffering)
Covenant   ⟷  Ritual (Sabbath)     (binding relation embodied in sacred time)
Covenant   ⟷  Transmission         (witnessed words preserved as binding memory)
Cosmology  ⟷  Apocalypse           (world structure disclosed, ruptured, renewed)
```

Sanctuary, Apocalypse, and Covenant are the three high-valence hubs. Genealogy stands alone. Mountain and Transmission are leaf nodes (one connection each).

### 1.3 Supporting (non-AO) commentary records — 36 total

- **11 genealogical chains** — all in Genesis (gen.4.25 → gen.48.5). The chains do their work within the patriarchal narrative and stop at the death of Joseph. Nothing yet covers Davidic lineage, Levitical priesthood lines, post-exilic genealogies, or the Matthean / Lukan messianic genealogies as discrete chambers (though AO·003 anchors at luk.3.38).
- **5 Doré plates** — all in Genesis 1-11 (creation of light, expulsion, Cain/Abel, deluge, Babel). Five plates of one Victorian engraver in one book. Decorative weight, no apparatus elsewhere.
- **~20 scholarly apparatus records on Genesis 1 alone** — cosmology, linguistic, manuscript, commentary (Augustine, Philo, Aquinas, Barth), cross-reference, reception, architecture, timeline, map, artifact, reconstruction, audio. This is the codex's **showpiece chapter** — and it currently has no peer.

### 1.5 Canonical density observations

**Heavy clusters (the gravity centres):**
- **Genesis 1** — apparatus showpiece (~20 records) + 2 originating AOs.
- **Genesis 1-17** — six AO originating anchors in a single book section.
- **Exodus 19-40** — four AO recurrence anchors (Sinai, tabernacle, sacred time).
- **Isaiah 6 / Ezekiel 1** — twin opened-heavens / sanctuary vision cluster.
- **Revelation 4 + 21-22** — eschatological convergence: six AOs terminate here (mountain, covenant, vision, incarnation, resurrection, sanctuary). The eschaton is **functioning as the codex's confluence point**.

**Sparse zones (ordinary narrative plains):**
- Numbers, Deuteronomy, Joshua, Judges, Ruth, 1-2 Samuel, 2 Kings, 2 Chronicles, Ezra, Nehemiah, Esther — the entire Deuteronomistic / Chronicler corpus is silent.
- All twelve minor prophets — completely silent.
- Mark — silent. The other three gospels have anchors; Mark does not.
- Acts — silent.
- The Pauline corpus (Rom, 2 Cor, Gal, Eph, Php, Col, 1-2 Th, 1-2 Tim, Tit, Phm) — only one anchor across thirteen letters (1 Cor 15:42).
- The Catholic epistles (1-2 Pet, 1-2-3 John, Jude) — silent.
- Song of Songs — silent.

**Genuinely missing biblical architectures:**

These are *patterns the canon clearly bears* that the current twelve do not articulate:

1. **The Exodus shape** — going-out from bondage; Egypt → Sinai → Wilderness → Promised Land → Exile → Return → Eschatological exodus. The single most repeated *macro-plot* in scripture. AO·005 (mountain) and AO·012 (sanctuary) gesture at it; nothing names the going-out itself.
2. **Sacrificial substitution** — the Lamb. Passover (exo.12), Day of Atonement (lev.16), Suffering Servant (isa.53), Lamb of God (jhn.1.29), Hebraic atonement (heb.9), Apocalyptic Lamb (rev.5). A through-line as strong as covenant.
3. **The Anointed One** — kingship. David (1sa.16, 2sa.7), royal psalms (psa.2, psa.110), branch / shoot prophecy (isa.11, jer.23), messianic son of man (dan.7), Matthean genealogy (mat.1), Acts kerygma (act.2), apocalyptic rider (rev.19). The messianic line.
4. **The divine council** — Yahweh in the assembly. psa.82, job.1-2, 1ki.22, dan.7. Rare but pivotal; a discrete shape.
5. **The pilgrim ascent** — going up to Jerusalem. Psalms of ascent (120-134), pilgrimage festivals, Mary and Joseph in luk.2, the Lord's own ascent. Often eclipsed by mountain (AO·005) but distinct: ascent is the *journey*, not the *summit*.
6. **Wilderness / wandering** — forty years, forty days; testing, manna, hidden formation. Currently invisible in the codex.
7. **Priesthood mediation** — Aaronic, Melchizedek, Christ as priest (heb.7). Could stand alone but partially overlaps with sanctuary.
8. **Purity / holiness gradation** — clean / unclean; the great Leviticus rhythm. Overlaps significantly with sanctuary's concentric layers (AO·012 already encodes the gradient in spatial form). May not need its own ontology.
9. **Inheritance** — naḥalah; promised land, kingdom inheritance, the Spirit as deposit. Diffuse; probably commentary.
10. **Martyrdom / witness** — Abel → prophets → Stephen → Antipas → the souls under the altar. Resonates with lament; possibly its own structure.

## PHASE 2 — Density and Pacing Audit

### 2.1 Does the codex still breathe?

**Yes, but unevenly.** The codex breathes well in two distinct ways and poorly in one:

- **It breathes well in sparsity.** After the v57 silent-folio refinement, a chapter without anchored witnesses now renders as undisturbed scripture. Psalms 1-12, 14-21, 23-103 — these are entire stretches of reading where the codex withdraws completely. That is correct restraint and should be preserved.
- **It breathes well at convergence.** Revelation 21-22 carrying six AO recurrence anchors is not over-saturation — it is the canon's actual convergence point. The codex is honouring the canon's own shape.
- **It does not breathe well across the Genesis 1 stack.** Genesis 1 carries ~20 supporting apparatus records (cosmology, linguistic, manuscript, commentary from Augustine / Philo / Aquinas / Barth, reception, architecture, audio cantillation). No other chapter has anything approaching that density. The contrast between Genesis 1 and Genesis 2 is currently a cliff, not a gradient.

### 2.2 Verses where multiple AOs converge

Cross-checking every anchor pid against itself:

| Verse        | AOs anchoring                                                                          |
|--------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| gen.1.6      | AO·001 (cosmology) + AO·002 (translation)                                              |
| isa.6.1      | AO·008 (vision) + AO·012 (sanctuary)                                                   |
| rev.21.3     | AO·006 (covenant) + AO·010 (incarnation)                                               |
| rev.21.5–22  | AO·005 + AO·011 + AO·012 (mountain, resurrection, sanctuary — adjacent verses, but no single verse holds three) |

No verse currently bears three AOs. This is by quiet design. **The two-AO ceiling per verse should be treated as load-bearing.** A triple-stack of markers at a single verse risks tipping the manuscript register toward annotation saturation.

### 2.3 Where commentary cadence currently feels strongest

- **Genesis 1** — the codex's most realized chamber; the full apparatus is present.
- **Genesis 5** — the antediluvian genealogy chamber: a lineage rendered as an inhabited room.
- **Exodus 40** — the sanctuary chamber: concentric narrowing as encounter form.
- **Psalm 13** — the lament chamber: structural breath, threshold and held silence.
- **John 1** — the incarnation chamber: concentric presence in flesh.

Five chambers are sufficient. The codex's range of forms has been established; it does not need to be re-established in another fifty chapters.

### 2.4 Where commentary cadence feels weakest

- **Most of Genesis (chapters 2-4, 6-49)** carries no apparatus beyond the genealogy chambers — yet sits inside a book the codex has marked as its showpiece. This raises an expectation the rest of Genesis does not meet.
- **Doré plates (5 in Genesis 1-11)** are an unrepeated style. Five Victorian engravings of one book is either the start of a print-illustration tradition or an orphan layer. It should be decided which.
- **Resonance pair count (8) is acceptable** but skews toward the Sanctuary/Incarnation/Apocalypse triad (3 of 8 pairs touch Sanctuary alone). The Sanctuary chamber surfaces three resonances — which the v54 diagnostic notes is "the maximum permitted per chamber." That maximum is right; do not raise it.

## PHASE 3 — Object Threshold Review

For each missing architecture identified in §1.5, a classification:

### A. Full Atlas Object candidate

Only architectures that meet *all five* criteria — unique manuscript rhythm, distinct encounter logic, civilizational architecture, sparse-but-powerful recurrence, non-overlap with existing objects — qualify.

| Candidate                          | Rationale                                                                                                                                                                                 | Tentative siglum |
|------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------|
| **The Exodus Shape**               | The single largest macro-plot in scripture. Going-out from bondage. Not captured by mountain (the summit) or sanctuary (the dwelling). Encounter logic: *threshold crossed*. Strong civilizational reach (Egyptian bondage, Babylonian exile, eschatological exodus from Babel/Babylon). | AO · 013         |
| **The Lamb Who Bears**             | Sacrificial substitution. Six clear recurrence anchors (exo.12, lev.16, isa.53, jhn.1.29, heb.9, rev.5). Encounter logic: *the cost transferred*. Distinct from covenant (which seals) and incarnation (which embodies).                                                                | AO · 014         |
| **The Anointed One**               | Kingship and messianic continuity. Eight-anchor through-line from David to the eschatological rider. Encounter logic: *the appointed one set apart*. Distinct from sanctuary (place) and incarnation (substance).                                                                       | AO · 015         |

These three would bring the codex to **fifteen objects**. The bar for AO·016+ should be deliberately *higher than* the bar for AO·013-015, because at fifteen the constellation is already saturated.

### B. Commentary-level motif only

These are real, but they belong as commentary leaves within chapters, not as ontology classes:

- **Priesthood mediation** — overlaps with sanctuary; deserves rich commentary at exo.28, lev.8-10, heb.7, but not its own chamber.
- **Inheritance / naḥalah** — diffuse; carries best through Leviticus 25, Joshua 13-21, Ephesians 1 commentary.
- **Temple economics (tithes, jubilee, gleaning)** — social-architectural; commentary at lev.25, deu.14, rut.2.
- **Purity systems** — overlaps with sanctuary's concentric structure; commentary at lev.11-15, mar.7, act.10.

### C. Resonance-only concept

These exist already through pairings between existing objects, or could be added as pair-glosses:

- **Covenant succession** — already in AO·006 (Abrahamic → Mosaic → New).
- **Sacred geography** — Eden / Promised Land / Jerusalem / New Earth is already carried by mountain + sanctuary's resonance pair.
- **Creation / New creation continuity** — already carried by Cosmology ↔ Apocalypse pair.
- **Holiness gradation** — already encoded in Sanctuary's five-layer concentric form.
- **Wilderness / wandering** — could be added as a *resonance gloss* between Exodus-Shape (if introduced as AO·013) and Lament.

### D. Should remain silent

Atlas restraint is stronger than annotation here. Some architectures are best left for the reader to encounter without the codex stepping into them:

- **The divine council** — has profound moments (psa.82, job.1, dan.7) but the canon itself treats it elusively. A chamber would over-clarify what scripture preserves in mystery. *Better left silent.*
- **Martyrdom** — too closely entangled with lament. Adding a martyrdom AO would compete with AO·009. The lament chamber already gestures at the rupture. *Better as commentary at rev.6.9-11.*
- **Pilgrim ascent** — distinct from mountain in principle but in practice the psalms of ascent and luk.2 carry the *experience* of ascent better than a chamber could. *Better as commentary at the psalms of ascent and exo.34.*

### Threshold summary

```
Future AO candidates that pass the bar:        3   (Exodus, Lamb, Anointed)
Architectures that belong as commentary:       4   (priesthood, inheritance, economics, purity)
Architectures already carried by resonance:    5   (succession, geography, new creation, holiness, wilderness)
Architectures that should remain silent:       3   (council, martyrdom, ascent)
```

**Final object ceiling proposed: 15.** No object beyond AO·015 should be considered until the codex has lived in 15-object form for at least one full reading cycle. The threshold for AO·016 should be: *can the canon be read in full without it feeling like an omission?* If yes, do not build it.

## PHASE 4 — Long-Form Reading Analysis

### 4.1 Reading Genesis for hours

**Current state:** the showpiece treatment of Genesis 1 makes the first chapter feel like a study session. Chapters 2-4 fall away to genealogy stubs at gen.4.25. Chapters 5-50 carry a steady but quiet stream of genealogical chambers + the five Doré plates clustered in 1-11. After Genesis 17 (the covenant anchor) the AO field thins; the reader rides on genealogies and one Doré plate.

**Risk:** the Genesis 1 stack sets an expectation of apparatus density the rest of the book cannot meet. A reader entering chapter 2 may feel the codex has *receded* rather than that scripture has *remained unbroken*.

**Editorial recommendation:** either trim Genesis 1 to the eight or ten records that are genuinely load-bearing, *or* commit to a second realized chamber elsewhere in Genesis (Genesis 22 — the Akedah — would be the strongest candidate, anchoring the Lamb if AO·014 is built, and bridging cleanly to Isaiah 53 and John 1). The cliff between Gen 1 and Gen 2 needs either to flatten or to be repeated as a deliberate rhythm.

### 4.2 Reading Psalms devotionally

**Current state:** four anchors across 150 chapters. Most psalms read as undisturbed scripture, with the wisdom-cadence (1.30em margin-bottom) gently spacing the verses. The lament chamber at Psalm 13 is genuinely realized.

**Risk:** none, currently. The codex's instinct here is correct. Devotional reading needs silence; the codex provides it.

**Editorial recommendation:** **stabilize Psalms now.** Do not add commentary records to psa.23, psa.51, psa.90, psa.119 — those are precisely the psalms whose devotional weight depends on the codex *not* speaking. The 22:1 anchor (lament recurrence) is enough.

### 4.3 Reading Isaiah slowly

**Current state:** two anchors (6.1, 7.14). Isaiah 53 — the Suffering Servant — has no Atlas Object yet, because the Lamb (AO·014) has not been built.

**Risk:** Isaiah is currently *under-served*. The book carries three of the strongest civilizational architectures in scripture (the Holy One, the Servant, the New Heavens / New Earth) and the codex touches only the throne vision.

**Editorial recommendation:** Isaiah is the single book that would gain most from the proposed AO·014 (Lamb) — and the originating anchor for the Lamb should arguably be isa.53.5, not exo.12, given the codex's existing structure. Reconsider when AO·014 is built.

### 4.4 Reading John meditatively

**Current state:** two anchors (1.14, 20.27). The incarnation chamber is realized; the resurrection chamber recurs here.

**Risk:** John 6 (bread of life), John 10 (good shepherd), John 14-17 (farewell discourse) carry distinct architectures the codex does not touch. Yet John's meditative reading depends precisely on *not* being interrupted. The codex's instinct (silent across most of the book) is probably right.

**Editorial recommendation:** consider one further anchor — jhn.1.29 ("the Lamb of God") — if AO·014 is built. Otherwise, leave John as it is.

### 4.5 Reading Revelation continuously

**Current state:** five AOs converge at chs. 21-22 (mountain, covenant, incarnation, resurrection, sanctuary), with opened-heavens recurring at 4:1. The eschaton is functioning as the codex's confluence point.

**Risk:** the chapter-22 stack is dense, but the rest of Revelation (chs. 5-20 — the seals, the trumpets, the beasts, the bowls) is silent. Reading Revelation continuously feels like reading toward a destination; the apocalyptic body of the book itself is not yet inhabited.

**Editorial recommendation:** consider — but only after AO·014 (Lamb) — anchoring AO·014 at rev.5.6. The slain Lamb at the throne is the structural centre of Revelation; the codex currently passes through it in silence.

## PHASE 5 — Strategic Roadmap

### 5.1 Current strengths

1. **Restraint is real.** Twelve objects across sixty-six books. The codex has resisted the temptation to surface itself at every motif.
2. **Originating gravity is honoured.** Every object has a clear originating anchor and behaves differently there than at its echoes.
3. **The five realized chambers** (Genesis 1, Genesis 5, Exodus 40, Psalm 13, John 1) are each materially distinct in their encounter grammar. The codex is not a uniform overlay.
4. **The convergence at Revelation 21-22 honours the canon's own shape** — the codex is not imposing a junction; it is sitting at the canon's actual confluence.
5. **The empty-folio silence (v57) is correct.** Absence is now a state, not a notice.
6. **The genre cadence stamp (v57) is felt-not-displayed** — Psalms breathe, prophets densify, Revelation pauses, narrative holds the baseline. The codex's atmosphere now varies by canonical register without announcing itself.

### 5.2 Greatest editorial dangers

1. **The Genesis 1 cliff.** Twenty apparatus records on chapter 1, near-silence everywhere else in Genesis. The contrast sets expectations the rest of the canon will not meet.
2. **Mark, Acts, and the Pauline corpus** as silent fields. Together they are nearly a quarter of the New Testament. The codex's silence here is *not* the restful silence of Leviticus or 2 Kings — it is the silence of a missing voice. The codex currently behaves as if the apostolic narrative arc does not exist between the Gospels and Revelation.
3. **The five Doré plates as an orphan layer.** Either expand the print-illustration tradition into a second cluster elsewhere (e.g., Job, Psalms, Isaiah) or stabilize at five and treat them as a Genesis-specific atmosphere.
4. **All twelve AOs at tertiary authority.** The CORE / STUDY / ARCHIVE depth filter is currently filtering by *kind*, not by *depth*. Either promote a small subset (perhaps three) to *primary* status to make the filter mean something, or remove the depth distinction.
5. **Sanctuary hub-saturation.** Three of eight resonance pairs touch Sanctuary. Treat the per-chamber resonance ceiling (three) as a permanent constraint.
6. **The next-AO trap.** Adding AO·013-015 will be the strongest temptation for the next six months. Each must be earned individually, not built as a set. *Do not build them in the same season.*

### 5.3 Most important unfinished biblical regions

In priority order, where the codex genuinely owes the canon a presence:

1. **Isaiah 53** — the Suffering Servant. Currently silent. (Awaits AO·014.)
2. **Exodus 12-15** — Passover and the crossing. The codex's macro-plot (going-out) currently has no anchor at its originating event. (Awaits AO·013.)
3. **2 Samuel 7** — the Davidic covenant. The royal architecture currently has no foothold. (Awaits AO·015.)
4. **Genesis 22** — the Akedah. The single most resonant passage between Genesis and the rest of the canon, currently silent.
5. **Mark / Acts / Pauline corpus** — at minimum, a small number of commentary records that re-establish the apostolic narrative arc as inhabited rather than empty. Not new AOs — modest commentary anchors at acts.2 (Pentecost), rom.8, eph.2, php.2 (the kenosis hymn).
6. **The minor prophets** — twelve silent books. A single recurrence anchor each from AO·006 (covenant) and AO·009 (lament) at appropriate passages (e.g., hos.6.1, mic.6.8, hab.2.4) would restore voice without building new objects.

### 5.4 Which future AO concepts are strongest

Re-stated from §3-A, in descending order of inevitability:

1. **AO · 014 — The Lamb Who Bears.** Strongest. Six recurrence anchors. Distinct encounter logic (substitution). Civilizational reach across Passover, Day of Atonement, Servant, Lamb of God, Apocalyptic Lamb. The codex's most conspicuous current omission.
2. **AO · 013 — The Exodus Shape.** Strong. The macro-plot. Encounter logic of threshold-crossing. Risk: overlap with sanctuary (the dwelling at the journey's end) and mountain (the summit en route). Must be rendered as *motion through liminality*, not as a destination.
3. **AO · 015 — The Anointed One.** Strong. Kingship is undeniable. Risk: overlap with incarnation (which is *who* the anointed one is). Must be rendered as *appointment and authority*, not as identity.

If only one is built in the next phase, it should be **AO·014 (Lamb)**. If two are built, AO·014 then AO·013. AO·015 should wait the longest — the messianic overlap with incarnation needs prolonged consideration.

### 5.5 Which systems should now stabilize permanently

The following should be treated as *closed* — not iterated on, not extended:

- **The depth filter (CORE / STUDY / ARCHIVE)** — either commit to using it via authority promotions or remove it. Do not refine its UI.
- **The resonance ceiling per chamber (three)** — permanent.
- **The encounter-memory localStorage key** — permanent. Do not extend memory toward recommendation or reading-history features.
- **The chamber grammar dispatch** — closed. Each new AO adds a chamber; no further refactoring of the existing dispatcher.
- **The codex preface (nine rubrics)** — closed. Do not add a tenth rubric.
- **The CSS pseudo-element marker glyphs** — closed. New AOs use new glyphs but no new mechanism.
- **The chapter-room architecture** — closed. The folio-column sidebar is the canonical reading view.

### 5.6 What "finishing the Bible" realistically means for Atlas

It does *not* mean:
- Markers on every chapter.
- A commentary leaf on every motif.
- Apparatus density approaching the Genesis 1 stack across the canon.
- A graph view, a dashboard, a search index of objects.
- An AO for every recognisable biblical pattern.

It *does* mean:
- **Fifteen Atlas Objects at most.** Three more (AO·013, 014, 015), built deliberately over the course of a year, not a season.
- **Five further realized chambers** of supporting commentary — Genesis 22 (Akedah), Exodus 12 (Passover), Isaiah 53 (Servant), Psalm 23 (or *no* Psalm 23 — the codex must decide), Romans 8. No chamber to match the Genesis 1 stack in density; each at perhaps eight to twelve records.
- **A single thin commentary anchor across each currently-silent book**, restoring voice without colonizing the silence — Numbers 6 (Aaronic blessing), Deuteronomy 6 (Shema), Judges 5 (Song of Deborah), Ruth 1, 1 Samuel 3, Ecclesiastes 1, Habakkuk 2:4, Acts 2, Romans 8, Ephesians 2, Philippians 2, 1 John 4. Twelve anchors across twelve currently-silent books. Each anchor a single commentary record, no more.
- **The eschatological convergence at Revelation 21-22 left exactly as it stands.** No additions, no subtractions. The confluence is correct.
- **Permanent silence preserved** in the divine council passages, the martyrdom passages, the pilgrim psalms. The codex's restraint there is the codex's voice.

## Closing posture

Atlas has crossed from experimental codex into civilizational manuscript. The temptation now is to keep building. The harder discipline is to keep refusing.

What the codex owes the Bible in the next year:

- **Three more objects, earned individually.**
- **Five more realized chambers.**
- **A dozen single-record commentary anchors across the silent books.**
- **Everything else: silence.**

A reader should be able to read from Genesis through to Revelation, over weeks or months, and feel — without being told — that the codex was always there. A book that has been kept.

The goal is inevitability.
