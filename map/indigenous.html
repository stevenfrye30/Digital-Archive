The Map of Indigenous & African Texts

The sacred oral traditions of Africa, the diaspora, Oceania, and Native America — mostly unwritten, living faiths whose “texts” are colonial-era transcriptions of oral practice. Coloured by the copyright of the published transcription — with the traditions that should not be mapped shown as such.

11 of 22 green — the green is the pre-1931 transcribers of living oral tradition — Callaway’s Zulu, the Bleek–Lloyd |Xam archive, Rattray’s Ashanti, Queen Liliʻuokalani’s Kumulipo, and the young Rastafari scriptures — while the deepest sacred material stays grey, unmapped by design.

Public-domain is a fact about a book, not permission from a community. Almost none of these traditions canonize a scripture; their sacred knowledge is oral, and often initiatory or restricted. This map colours the copyright status of already-published ethnographic transcriptions — it indexes what exists, it does not reproduce restricted content. The green is largely the work of transcribers who recorded oral tradition before 1931 (Callaway’s Zulu, the Bleek–Lloyd |Xam archive, Queen Liliʻuokalani’s Kumulipo). The grey is the honest part: the Australian Aboriginal Dreaming, Haitian Vodou, Cuban Santería and Brazilian Candomblé are deliberately not mapped — living, oral, and held rather than printed — and the Walam Olum is a debunked forgery. Those absences are the finding.

African Traditional the orisha, the ancestors & the diviners8 texts

Pacific & Oceanic the atua, the mana & the creation chants4 texts

The religions of Polynesia — the least ethically fraught pillar, because the tradition-bearers often held the pen themselves. Queen Liliʻuokalani translated the Kumulipo during her imprisonment; Māori and Hawaiian narrators shaped their own transcriptions.

Native North America living & often sacred-restricted4 texts

The most delicate pillar. Public-domain ethnography exists, but several items are ceremonial texts the originating nations actively wish were not in circulation — copyright-free is not the same as ethically free. These are flagged, not celebrated.
