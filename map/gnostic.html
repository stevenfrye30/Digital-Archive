Mead’s recoveries (Pistis Sophia, Fragments of a Faith Forgotten) are public-domain — the red is everything dug from the sand in the twentieth century (Nag Hammadi, the Manichaean and Mandaean codices), still under copyright.

What the heresy-hunters quoted survives; what the heretics wrote does not. Gnostic religion reaches us two ways. The first is public-domain: the Church Fathers’ hostile summaries (the Ante-Nicene Fathers, 1867–85) and the recoveries of G.R.S. Mead (Pistis Sophia 1896, Fragments of a Faith Forgotten 1900) — a luminous green core. The second is a red frontier: the heretics’ own books, buried in the sand and dug up only in the twentieth century — the entire Nag Hammadi library (1945), the Manichaean codices (1930), the Gospels of Mary and Judas — every one still under copyright. Living Mandaeism, the last Gnostic religion, has no public-domain English scripture at all. The mystery-cult neighbours (the Hermetica, the Mithras Liturgy, the Orphic Hymns, the Chaldean Oracles) live on the Ancient World door.

Manichaeism Mani’s world religion · 3rd c. CE, Rome to China5 texts

Once a rival to Christianity from North Africa to China, now wholly extinct. Mani wrote his own scriptures — and almost none survive in public-domain English; the church is visible mainly through the Fathers who condemned it.

Mandaeism the last living Gnostics · Iraq & Iran3 texts

The only Gnostic religion to survive to the present day — and, strikingly, the one with no public-domain English scripture at all. Its holy books exist in English only in modern copyrighted or German editions.
