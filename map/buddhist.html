The Map of Buddhist Texts

One root that split into three great canons — the Pāli, Chinese, and Tibetan — coloured by whether a worldwide-public-domain English translation exists. Start with the whole tradition, then walk into any canon.

The light fades as you move north & east

Each canon’s share of green / amber / red. The oldest, southernmost canon is the most translated; the tradition’s expansion outran translation.

green — worldwide-PD or CC0 (ingestible) amber — © / open-access-only / US-PD but not yet worldwide red — no standard English translation
The exact inverse of Hinduism. There, the ancient core was green and the edges went amber. Here the oldest, southernmost canon is the greenest — the entire Pāli Sutta Piṭaka is CC0 (Sujato/SuttaCentral) on top of a century of public-domain PTS & SBE scholarship — and the map darkens as the tradition moved north and east, because the expansion into Mahāyāna and Vajrayāna generated vaster literatures (2,920 Chinese texts, ~4,000 Tibetan treatises) faster than anyone could render them. The frontier here isn’t copyright — it’s the untranslated ocean: the Tibetan Tengyur isn’t projected to be finished in English until 2110. Tap ▸ open on any pillar to walk into it.
