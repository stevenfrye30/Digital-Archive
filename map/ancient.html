The Map of Ancient World Texts

The public domain runs greenest at the dawn of history. Most of the ancient world’s sacred literature is freely readable — not only because it is ancient, but because the Victorian and Edwardian antiquarians (Budge, Breasted, L.W. King, Frazer, Mead, Thorpe, Lady Guest, Brinton, Markham) translated nearly all of it into the public domain before 1931. The red gaps trace a sharp historical line: the great corpora deciphered too late — the Ugaritic Baal Cycle and the Hittite myths (read only after 1930) — are wholly under copyright, as are the Egyptian Pyramid & Coffin Texts (the oldest religious writing on Earth, first Englished in 1952 and 1973) and the Maya Popol Vuh, which astonishingly still has no complete public-domain English. The gods of these traditions live in the comparative-pantheon map; here are their books.
