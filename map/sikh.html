6 of 15 green — the Guru Granth Sahib reads green through Macauliffe’s 1909 Sikh Religion and Trumpp’s 1877 Ādi Granth; the Dasam Granth and companion texts are mostly modern ©.

One book, treated as a living Guru. The Guru Granth Sahib (1,430 pages, 31 rāgas, 5,894 hymns) gathers the poetry of six Gurus and fifteen Hindu & Muslim bhagats. Its green anchor is Max Arthur Macauliffe’s six-volume The Sikh Religion (1909), which translated great swaths of it into public-domain English — but there is no complete worldwide-PD English Guru Granth (the complete modern translations, including the ubiquitous Sant Singh Khalsa, are all copyright). Past the scripture, the contested Dasam Granth and the companion texts stay mostly amber.

The primary scripture, compiled by Guru Arjan (1604, scribed by Bhai Gurdas) and invested as the eternal Guru by Guru Gobind Singh (1708). Macauliffe’s 1909 work is the public-domain window into it.
