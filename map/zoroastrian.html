48 of 53 green — the Avesta (Gāthās, Yasna, 21 Yašts, Vendidād, Khordeh Avesta) and the Pahlavi books, almost all in the Sacred Books of the East.

The purest Sacred-Books-of-the-East door in the archive. Almost the entire canon was translated between 1880 and 1887 by three scholars — Darmesteter (the Vendidād and Yašts), Mills (the Yasna, Visperad and Gāthās), and E.W. West (the Pahlavi books) — all long in the public domain. So Zoroastrianism, though one of the world’s oldest revealed religions, is also one of the greenest: nearly every text is freely readable. Only the modern verse retranslations of the Gāthās and the late Persian Rivāyats stay amber.

The Gāthās the hymns of Zarathustra himself6 texts

The oldest layer — 17 hymns composed by Zarathustra (~1200 BCE), in the archaic Gathic dialect, embedded within the Yasna. Grouped into five Gāthās by metre. All public-domain via Mills (SBE 31); only the modern verse retranslations are ©.

The Yasna & Visperad the chief liturgy3 texts

The Yasna is the primary act of worship, recited at the Yajishn ceremony; the Visperad extends it for the high feasts. The Yasna Haptaŋhāiti is an archaic prose core nearly as old as the Gāthās.

The Vendidād 22 fargards · the law against the demons3 texts

The Vidēvdād ("law against the demons") — the priestly code of purity and its mythic frame, including the tale of Yima and the vara (an ark-like refuge). The only Avestan book preserved complete with its Zand.

The Pahlavi Books 9th-c. theology · E.W. West, SBE 5/18/24/37/4712 texts

The great Middle-Persian theological literature, written as Islam rose — the systematic doctrine, cosmology, law, and apocalyptic of Zoroastrianism. E.W. West translated nearly all of it across five SBE volumes (all PD).
